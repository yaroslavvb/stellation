Stellation, in one minute

Take a pentagon and extend its five sides. They cross again, and the crossings outline a five-pointed star. That is a stellation: the same lines, followed further.

In three dimensions you extend a polyhedron's face planes instead. The planes slice space into small bounded regions — cells. Every choice of cells that respects the solid's symmetry is a stellation of it. The icosahedron famously has 59 of them.

How to use this

  • Pick a solid on the left. The centre shows it, and to its right is the stellation diagram: one face plane with every other face plane's trace drawn across it.
  • Each little region in that diagram is a cell. Click one to add or remove it, and the solid updates.
  • Cells come in layers, numbered outward from the core. The buttons core only and + layer walk out one shell at a time — layer 0 alone is the original polyhedron, layer 0+1 is its first stellation.
  • A cell marked L/R is chiral: it comes as a mirror pair, and you may take one hand.
  • Export the result as STL for printing, or save the selection as a .stel file in the original program's format.

Where this comes from

This is a JavaScript port of the Stellation applet written by Vladimir Bulatov in 1998–2001. The geometry here is a direct port of his algorithm, checked against the original Java: the same facet counts, the same cell structure, the same volumes to six decimal places.
